Flex Academy

Landing page for a new academy that teaches people how to start and grow a short-term rental business. Design test assignment.

Constraint
Two audiences and two offers on one page, prices not set yet, an audience that distrusts online courses, and a brand that had to feel related to The Flex without copying it.
Decision
Build the page around beginners, the bigger group, and give experienced owners a clear but quiet path of their own. Show the real work instead of selling a dream.
Trade-off
One page for two audiences means one of them comes second. Experienced owners come second, so some of them may miss their path. That is the first thing I'd check after launch.
Role
Sole designer, from brief to high fidelity
Timeframe
5 days
Context
Design test assignment for The Flex and Base360. The Flex runs furnished apartments in 7 cities, and Base360 is the software its founders built to manage them. Flex Academy is their new company, and this page is its front door.
Outcome
A full desktop landing page, mobile versions of the key sections, both booking flows with all their states, and a walkthrough video explaining the decisions.

The brief

Flex Academy has two programmes. Launch is for people starting their first rental, and it's sold through a free webinar. Scale is for people who already run a few units and want to grow, and it's sold through a free strategy call with the team.

The brief set three goals for the page. A visitor should understand in 5 seconds who it's for and what they get, trust it within 30 seconds, and be able to act at any point without searching for a button. I designed the page, the mobile version, and both booking flows: the webinar sign-up and the strategy call booking.

Where I started

Before designing anything, I did a task analysis in FigJam. I broke the brief down into the company, the goal, the problem, the audience, the founders' expectations and what I had to deliver. Then I made a plan for the 5 days, so I knew what to design and in what order.

Two audiences, one page

The brief named two audiences. The task analysis helped me understand them better, and that's where my main decision came from.

The first audience is people with a regular job who want to start their own rental business. They have no flat yet, or just one. This is the bigger group, so I decided to build the page around them and make the free webinar the main action everywhere.

The second audience already runs a few units and wants to turn them into a real company. They're stuck on manual work and have no time to grow. They need a different conversation, so they get their own path: the Scale programme and a strategy call.

The challenge was to serve both without making the page feel like two sites glued together.

The problem

The analysis left me with three problems the page had to solve:

  • Two audiences, one page. Beginners and experienced owners need different offers, and neither should feel the page is made for someone else.
  • Trust. Not everyone who lands here knows The Flex, and this audience doesn't trust "get rich" courses.
  • Speed. A visitor should understand in 5 seconds who the page is for and what they get, and be able to act at any point.

The solution: three key decisions

The brief asked me to explain three key decisions. Each one answers a problem above, and the rest of the page is built on them:

  • Both paths start in the same card. The webinar is the main button, because beginners are the bigger audience. People who already run units get a small link right under it, so they find their way without a second button competing for attention.
  • No guru style. This audience doesn't trust get-rich courses, so the page shows the real work, the founders admit their early mistakes, and the questions section says plainly that this is not passive income.
  • The call form sends beginners to the webinar. If someone books a strategy call but runs 0 or 1 units, the form suggests the webinar instead. This keeps the team's time for the right people.

The hero: understood in seconds

The goal for the hero was that nobody has to guess what this is. The headline, "Start your rental business with the playbook behind The Flex", says who it's for, and the line under it says what you get: find a flat, set it up and get it booked, with video lessons, live sessions and the team's templates.

The webinar card is the main thing in the hero. It shows the date, what you'll learn and one button, so you see the value before you click. Right under the button there's a small link for people who already run two or more units, which takes them to the Scale programme. Both audiences find their way in one place, and beginners see only one button.

I compared about six hero directions side by side before choosing this one. Most of them looked good but explained the offer too slowly, or showed flats in a way that felt more like Airbnb than a business.

Earning trust

Not everyone who lands here knows The Flex, and the audience doesn't trust "get rich" courses. So the page avoids that style completely: no income promises and no luxury lifestyle photos.

Instead, the founders tell their own story, including what went wrong at the start: they overpaid for furniture, double-booked guests and did all the cleaning themselves. Quiet links to The Flex and Base360 let anyone check that the company is real. The questions section answers "Is this passive income?" with an honest no.

Choosing a programme

Next, visitors choose where they are: "Starting out" or "Already running". The section changes with the answer.

For beginners, it shows what the course includes and a preview of the course itself, with lessons, progress and templates like a deal calculator. For experienced owners, it shows Base360, the software the company built. On the strategy call, the team shows how Base360 would work with your own units, which is one of the strongest reasons to choose Scale.

After that, a table puts both programmes side by side. When a page offers two things, people want to compare them, so the table answers "what do I actually get" without anyone reading twice.

The page ends with one more invitation to the webinar, a link to book a call, and a free checklist by email for people who aren't ready yet.

The booking flows

Both flows are short, because every extra step loses people.

The webinar sign-up asks for three things: name, email and country. Country is there so the time shows in your own time zone. Then you see a confirmation with the date and buttons to add it to your calendar.

The strategy call asks a few quick questions: how many units you run, where you want to be in a year, and your budget. Then you pick a time, see the name of the person you'll talk to, and get a confirmation.

If someone opens the call form and picks "0 to 1" units, the same form changes and suggests the webinar instead, because the call is made for people growing a portfolio. They can still book the call if they want. This keeps the team's time for the right people and saves beginners from a sales call they don't need yet.

Brand: part of the family, not a copy

Most of the brand comes from The Flex website: the green, the background colours, the text colours and the button corners. Someone who knows The Flex recognises the family straight away.

To give the academy its own character, I changed the type. Headlines are in a serif, which feels closer to a book or a course than a booking site. I also kept the page away from the software look of Base360. This is a place to learn, so it reads more like a magazine: big headlines, real photos and a personal story.

Mobile

On mobile, the hero stacks. The photo and headline come first, and the webinar card sits right under them, so the main button is on the first screen. The programme section keeps the same switch, and the course and software previews run edge to edge so they stay readable on a small screen.

Note on images

Hero photos are placeholders from cosmos.so. Prices and lesson counts are placeholders. The founders' photo is from the Base360 website.

What it was designed to change

The main metric: the share of visitors who register for the webinar or book a call. That's what the page exists for.

  • Conversion: how many visitors sign up for the webinar or book a strategy call.
  • Where people convert: which button they use (the hero, the programme section, the table or the end of the page). If almost nobody uses one of them, the page can be shorter.
  • Do experienced owners find their path: clicks on the Scale link in the hero compared with call bookings from people with two or more units. If they miss it, Scale needs a more visible way in.
  • The 5-second test: can a new visitor say who the page is for and what they'd get after looking at the hero for five seconds? That's the brief's own success test.
